Popular tourist attractions

The experiences visitors talk about most. Each one opens a full guide.

  • Ride the Kandy to Ella train

    Window seats on this hill-country line pass tea estates, waterfalls and cloud forest. Book reserved seats ahead where you can.

  • Look for leopards in Yala

    Yala is known for one of the highest leopard densities in the world, along with elephants, sloth bears and crocodiles.

  • Climb Sigiriya at first light

    Start early to climb the ancient stairways past frescoes to the summit gardens before the heat builds.

  • Go whale watching off Mirissa

    Blue whales and other species are seen from the south coast between roughly November and April.

  • Walk the ramparts of Galle Fort

    Circle the old Dutch walls in the late afternoon and finish at the lighthouse as the sun goes down.

  • Watch sunrise from Adam’s Peak

    Pilgrims and travellers climb thousands of steps overnight to reach the summit for dawn.

Why visit Sri Lanka

A compact island with unusually big contrasts.

🏛️

Eight UNESCO World Heritage Sites

Ancient capitals, a rock fortress, a sacred hill city, a colonial fort, rainforest and highlands, all on one small island.

🌊

Two coasts, two seasons

When the south and west coasts get monsoon rain, the east coast is usually dry, so there is a good beach somewhere for much of the year.

🐘

Wildlife within reach

Elephants, leopards, sloth bears and blue whales can all be seen within a day or two of travel from each other.

🍃

Hills and beaches, a day apart

Cool tea country and warm palm-fringed shores are a single scenic journey apart, by road or by rail.

About Sri Lanka

An island in the Indian Ocean with more than two thousand years of recorded history, home to about 22 million people.

Rich cultural heritage

Sinhalese, Tamil, Muslim, Burgher and other communities share the island. Buddhism, Hinduism, Islam and Christianity are all practised, and temples, kovils, mosques and churches often stand close together.

Ancient kingdoms

Anuradhapura became a royal capital in the 4th century BCE, followed by Polonnaruwa, Sigiriya and others. The Kingdom of Kandy was the last to fall, to the British in 1815.

Beautiful beaches

Palm-lined bays run along the south and west, while the east coast offers surf and quieter sands. Mirissa, Hikkaduwa and Arugam Bay each have a different mood.

Wildlife and national parks

More than twenty national parks protect elephants, leopards, sloth bears and hundreds of bird species. The seas off the south coast are among the best places to see blue whales.

Hill country and tea plantations

Commercial tea planting began in the 1860s, and Ceylon tea is still grown on the cool slopes around Nuwara Eliya, Hatton and Ella. Trains wind through the estates.

Traditional food and festivals

Expect rice and curry, hoppers, kottu roti, pol sambol and sweet treats. Festivals include Sinhala and Tamil New Year in April, Vesak in May, Thai Pongal in January and the Kandy Esala Perahera in July or August.
